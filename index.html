A simple guide through time

Palestine: from the Bronze Age to the present

This page gives a broad chronological overview of the peoples, rulers and political changes in the land historically known as Palestine. It is a starting point rather than a complete history.

Dates are approximate. Names, borders and identities changed over time, so modern political labels should not be projected unchanged into every ancient period.

🏺

c. 3300–1200 BCE

Bronze Age: Canaanite city-states

The region was home to many Canaanite-speaking communities living in towns, villages and fortified cities. It stood between Egypt, Syria and Mesopotamia and was shaped by trade, migration and competing empires.

🛡️

c. 1200–586 BCE

Iron Age: local kingdoms and larger empires

Philistine settlements developed along parts of the southern coast, while the kingdoms of Israel and Judah emerged inland. Assyrian and later Babylonian expansion transformed the political map of the region.

🏛️

586–332 BCE

Babylonian and Persian periods

After Babylon conquered Judah and destroyed the First Temple, the region later became part of the Persian Achaemenid Empire. Communities with different religious and local traditions continued to live across the area.

🏺

332–63 BCE

Hellenistic and Hasmonean rule

Alexander the Great's conquests brought the region into the Hellenistic world. Control later shifted among successor kingdoms, and the Hasmonean kingdom arose following the Maccabean revolt.

🏟️

63 BCE–638 CE

Roman and Byzantine periods

Rome absorbed the region into its imperial system. Jerusalem and the wider area experienced revolts, destruction, rebuilding and major religious change. Christianity became especially important during Byzantine rule.

🌙

638–1099

Early Islamic rule

Muslim armies took control in the 7th century. Palestine was governed under successive caliphates, while Muslim, Christian and Jewish communities all remained part of the region's social and religious life.

🏰

1099–1516

Crusader, Ayyubid and Mamluk periods

Crusader states were established after 1099. Saladin's Ayyubid forces later retook Jerusalem, and the Mamluks eventually ruled the area as part of a wider eastern Mediterranean system.

🕌

1516–1917

Ottoman Palestine

For roughly four centuries the region formed part of the Ottoman Empire. Most people lived in towns and villages, with agriculture, local trade, pilgrimage and links to neighbouring Arab cities shaping everyday life.

📜

1917–1948

British Mandate

Britain took control during the First World War. The period saw growing Palestinian Arab nationalism, expanding Zionist immigration and institution-building, land and political conflict, revolt and increasingly competing national projects.

🧳

1948–1967

War, Israel's creation and the Nakba

The 1948 war accompanied the establishment of Israel and the mass displacement of Palestinians, remembered by Palestinians as the Nakba. The West Bank came under Jordanian rule and the Gaza Strip under Egyptian administration.

🗺️

1967–1993

Occupation and the rise of the PLO

In the 1967 war Israel captured the West Bank, East Jerusalem and Gaza Strip. The Palestine Liberation Organization became the principal representative body of the Palestinian national movement, and the first Intifada began in 1987.

🤝

1993–2006

Oslo era and limited Palestinian self-government

The Oslo agreements created the Palestinian Authority and introduced limited self-rule in parts of the West Bank and Gaza. Final-status questions — including borders, settlements, Jerusalem and refugees — were left unresolved.

🚧

2007–2022

Political division, blockade and repeated conflict

Palestinian politics became geographically divided, with Hamas governing Gaza and the Palestinian Authority remaining dominant in parts of the West Bank. Israel and Egypt restricted movement in and out of Gaza, while settlement growth and periodic wars deepened the conflict.

🕊️

2023–present

War in Gaza and an unresolved political future

After the Hamas-led attacks in Israel on 7 October 2023, Israel launched a large-scale military campaign in Gaza. The period has brought enormous civilian suffering, displacement and destruction, while international debate has intensified over ceasefire, hostages, humanitarian access, accountability, Palestinian statehood and a long-term political settlement.
